Time and Task Management



There's never enough time to finish everything, but there's always time to finish the most important things. So be selective. You'll always be behind on some tasks anyway. Work on one single task until it's done. Single-tasking might be the most important practice in life. The key to success is action.

Prioritizing Tasks



Eat the frog: do the ugliest, most procrastinated-on task first, and resist the urge to start with the easiest one.

Three qualities form the habit of getting things done. All of them learnable:

  • Decision
  • Discipline
  • Determination

Visualize yourself as the person who gets important stuff done on time. You need a definite purpose and the desire to achieve it. Clarity is likely the most important concept in productivity: the more clarity, the more determination, the easier it is to eat the frog. So think on paper and write out exactly what you want. Unwritten goals are just wishes.

Goal Setting and Planning



  • Set deadlines or sub-deadlines.
  • Written goals drive achievement. The more you write about them, the stronger the desire to accomplish them.
  • An average plan with tasks done beats a perfect plan without action. Never miss a day.
  • Write down your ten most important goals, pick the one with the most impact on your life, and work on it every day.
  • Plan every day in advance.

Efficiency and Procrastination



Ten minutes of planning can save two hours of work. It's amazing how few people actually plan their work. Your subconscious works on the list overnight, which is one more reason to write the lists a day before:

  • Monthly list
  • Weekly list (updated as the week progresses)
  • Daily list the night before

Always work from the list. New items go back on the list, not into your head.

Project Management and Prioritization



Each project gets its own list, ordered by priority. Ten percent of planning makes the other ninety percent more efficient. The most valuable twenty percent of items provides eighty percent of the benefits, and it's also the hardest, so most people grab the easy eighty percent first and never get to the important stuff. Don't.

Think five, ten, twenty years ahead. What you do now has long-term consequences, not short-term gains. Unsuccessful people chase short-term goals and pleasures.

Whatever you plan, add twenty percent to the time estimate, and you may finish early and relaxed. Keep asking: what's the task with the highest value right now?

Effective Work Strategies



  • Do first things first and second things never.
  • Creative procrastination: deliberately put off the less critical ugly frogs.
  • You control your life and time only through what you say "no" to. Reject anything not of high value to your life, early and often.
  • Saying no frees time for new priorities, like family, reading, or sport.
  • People procrastinate on things they're bad at. Everyone has strengths and weaknesses. Set goals for the areas that need development.
  • Identify the skills with the biggest positive impact on your life and keep improving them. Continual improvement is what transforms your life.
  • Working effectively buys you a decent work-life balance and a better life at home.

Maintaining Productivity



Getting started is often the hardest part, so just start with the highest-value task of the day. A tidy desk helps. Prepare ahead, get eighty percent right, and correct the rest later. Don't be a perfectionist. Be ready to fail; you miss one hundred percent of the shots you don't take. Divide big frogs into many small tasks.

Continuous learning matters too. When you know how to do something, you procrastinate less. Use the commute to listen to educational audio. Decide to excel in your chosen field and enjoy it.

Don't wait for others to motivate you. Leaders work without supervision. Put pressure on yourself and pick your own frog.

Attitude and Technology Management



Meeting self-imposed deadlines builds the reputation you have with yourself. Whatever your mood, act cheerful and tell people you feel terrific. Optimists look for the positives in setbacks and hunt for solutions. Spend your energy on your goals, not on criticizing others.

Technology can be your best friend or your worst enemy. Treat it as a tool, not a master, and unplug regularly, say one day a week. No task switching. Don't check messages in the morning, and at most twice a day. Build a habit of two to three hours of focused work every morning.

The "Salami Slice" or "Swiss Cheese" method: slice daunting projects into smaller pieces. Urgency triggers high performance. Aim to accomplish three to five things in a workday.
